The sequence

This is the map. Not theory - the exact order I use so you can go from idea to a live page with a real Stripe payment link. Read it once. Then do Day 0.

How to use this page

Don’t try to finish everything in one sitting. The people who get stuck usually open five tabs, get overwhelmed, and quit before they publish anything.

Your only job for the first stretch is this: pick one simple offer, write a clear brief, build a clean page, and attach a payment link. That’s the win. Everything else can wait.

Tip: Keep a notes file open. Write the name of your offer, the one person it’s for, and the price. You’ll use those three lines in almost every step.

What you’re building

A small digital product or service with a page people can understand in under ten seconds, and a Stripe link that takes payment. PDF, template, short guide, audit, checklist, or a simple done-for-you setup - it doesn’t have to be complicated.

Day 0 - Get the accounts open

Before you design anything, open the three accounts. Do this even if you only finish one today. Momentum matters more than perfection.

1

Grok

Go to grok.com (or open Grok inside X). Create or log in. You’ll use it to turn rough ideas into clear product briefs and page prompts.

Tip: Treat Grok like a sharp assistant, not a magic button. The clearer you are about the outcome, the better the output.
2

Lovable

Go to lovable.dev and create an account. This is where the brief becomes a real page. Free credits are enough to learn the workflow and ship a first version.

Tip: One focused prompt beats ten vague ones. That’s why we build the PRD before we open Lovable.
3

Stripe

Go to Stripe and create an account. Complete the basics so you can create a Payment Link. You don’t need a full store - a single payment link is enough for your first offer.

Tip: Start in test mode if you want to practice. When the page looks right, switch to live and create the real link.

The pipeline (do it in this order)

Most people fail because they start in the middle - they open Lovable with a vague idea and hope it “looks professional.” We reverse that. Validate → angle → brief → build → payment.

1

Validate the market

Don’t invent a product for “everyone.” Pick a narrow problem someone already spends time or money trying to fix. Use the free Market Validator so you leave with a short brief instead of a vague feeling.

Open Market Validator →
Example: “Freelance designers who hate writing proposals” is better than “people who need AI help.” Narrow wins.
2

Find the offer angle

A market without an angle is just noise. The Offer Angle Finder turns a validated market into 2-3 concrete offer concepts: name, promise, page outline, and a sensible price band.

Open Offer Angle Finder →
Pick one angle. Commit for this first build. You can test a second angle later - after you have a live link.
3

Write the PRD with Grok

Open the PRD Prompt Builder. Answer the questions. Copy the output into Grok and ask it to tighten the page structure, headline options, and section order. You’re not asking Grok to “make a website.” You’re asking it for a clear product brief a builder can execute.

Open PRD Prompt Builder →

Then go to Grok and paste the brief. Ask for:

  • One strong homepage outline
  • Headline + subhead options
  • What the buyer gets in plain language
  • A short FAQ (3-5 questions)
Tip: If the output feels generic, add constraints: “No hype words. Speak to freelancers who are tired of tool-hopping. Keep sections short.”
4

Build the page in Lovable

Open Lovable. Start a new project. Paste the refined PRD / homepage brief as your first prompt. Let it generate the page, then iterate in small requests: fix spacing, strengthen the CTA, simplify the hero.

Goal for version one: a page that explains the offer, shows what’s included, and points to a buy button. It does not need every animation in the world.

Tip: After each change, read the page out loud. If a sentence feels salesy or unclear, rewrite it in plain language. Buyers trust clarity more than glow effects.
5

Create the Stripe payment link

In the Stripe Dashboard go to Payment Links → create a new link. Set the product name, price, and (if you want) a success redirect to a simple thank-you page or a Google Drive delivery folder.

Copy the payment link. Put it on your page as the main CTA - “Get access,” “Buy the guide,” whatever matches the offer. Test it yourself with a small amount or test mode first.

This is the moment the promise becomes real. A live page + a working payment link is the first win. Post that win in Skool - even if revenue is still zero. Shipping is the signal.
6

Publish and share

Publish from Lovable (or export and host if you’ve moved to GitHub / Cloudflare). Share the link with a small circle first: a friend, a group, or one post with a clear explanation of who it’s for.

You don’t need a big audience on day one. You need proof that the path works end to end.

First-week checklist

  • ☐ Grok, Lovable, and Stripe accounts open
  • ☐ One validated idea from Market Validator
  • ☐ One offer angle chosen
  • ☐ PRD written and refined in Grok
  • ☐ Page built in Lovable
  • ☐ Stripe payment link live and tested
  • ☐ Intro posted in Skool + what you’re building
